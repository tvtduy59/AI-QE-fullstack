# Accessibility and human judgment

Identify the agreed accessibility target (standard/version/level), supported platforms, and user needs. If none is specified, propose a scope for review rather than declaring a contractual conformance target. Do not provide legal compliance conclusions.

Use risk-based selections covering keyboard reachability and operation, focus visibility/order/restoration, accessible names/roles/states, labels and error guidance, semantic structure, contrast and non-color cues, zoom/reflow, motion/time limits, and assistive-technology workflows where applicable. Tie specific success criteria to the chosen WCAG version by checking the normative text; do not invent criterion IDs.

Plan automated checks plus human keyboard/visual/screen-reader evaluation. Automated tools cannot determine accessibility by themselves; manual review and relevant user involvement remain important. A clean scan is a limited result, not complete conformance. Preserve the tested pages/states, criterion scope, tool version, manual methods, exceptions, and unassessed areas.

Sources checked 2026-09-21: [W3C evaluation overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/) and [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). Use these as methodology sources, not automatically adopted product requirements.
